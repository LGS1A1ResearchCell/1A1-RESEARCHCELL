# Administrator guide

**1A1 Research Cell — research archive and publication platform**
Lahore Grammar School 1-A-1, Lahore

This guide is for the people who run the system: **Research Cell Leadership**
(Head of Research, Deputy Head of Research) and **School Administration**.

You do not need any technical knowledge to use it. If you are looking after the
software itself, read [HANDOVER.md](./HANDOVER.md) instead.

## Who can do what

There are three levels of access. That is the whole list.

**Student** — Research Cell members. They submit research, respond to feedback,
and edit their own profile.

**Research Cell Leadership** — the Head of Research and Deputy Head of Research.
Everything a student can do, plus: read every submission, record review
decisions, and assemble journals.

**School Administration** — everything above, plus: approve new accounts, change
someone's access, make a student profile public, approve and publish journals,
edit the site's wording, and read the activity log.

> **"Team Leader" is not a level of access.** When a team submits research, one
> student is the Team Leader for *that piece of work* — they upload the document
> and name the co-authors. It gives them no extra powers anywhere else in the
> system.

A few things are reserved for School Administration alone, and cannot be done by
leadership no matter how senior:

- approving or declining a new student account
- publishing a journal
- granting anyone access
- making a student's profile public

## Signing in

There are two sign-in pages:

- **Student Login** — for Research Cell members
- **Admin Login** — for leadership and school administration

Use Admin Login. Both check the same thing; your account decides what you can
see once you are in.

**If you cannot sign in:**

- If you have never set a password, you need the invite link from whoever
  approved your account.
- If you see *"Your account is suspended"* or *"pending"*, your account exists
  but is not active. Another school administrator can reactivate it.
- You cannot reactivate your own suspended account. That is deliberate.

## Approving new student accounts

*School administration only.* **Menu: New Student Accounts**

When a student registers on the site, **no account is created**. A request
arrives here for you to review. Nobody can sign in until you approve them.

Each request shows the student's name, email address, class (A1 or A2) and the
year they say they joined the Research Cell.

### To approve

1. Check the name and class are right, and that this is genuinely a Research
   Cell member.
2. Click **Approve**.
3. A **link appears on screen with a copy button.** Copy it.
4. **Send that link to the student.** They open it and choose their own password.

**The system does not email the link.** This is the one manual step in the whole
process. Until the school has set up email in the system, you have to pass the
link on yourself.

> Treat the link the way you would treat a password. Anyone who has it can set
> the password on that account. Hand it over in person, or through a channel the
> school already trusts — not a public group chat. It is for one student and one
> account.

You do not create passwords for students, and you never see theirs. They choose
their own.

### To decline

Click **Decline** and give a short reason. The reason is recorded. No account is
created.

## Managing accounts once they exist

*School administration only.* **Menu: People**

### Account status

- **Active** — can sign in and use the system normally.
- **Suspended** — can sign in, but reaches nothing. Use this for a member who
  should not have access at the moment.
- **Alumni** — the same restriction, for members who have left. Their name stays
  on their research permanently.

Suspending or marking someone alumni **never deletes their work**. Their name
stays on every paper they wrote, and every journal they appear in. That was a
deliberate design decision: the record of who wrote what does not depend on
whether they still have an account.

### Making a profile public

Student profiles are private by default. A profile only appears on the public
**People** page when a school administrator makes it public.

Neither the student nor Research Cell leadership can do this. It is reserved for
school administration, because it means putting a child's name and photograph on
a public website.

Published research is a separate matter: a **published paper always shows its
authors' names**, whether or not those students have public profiles. Publishing
the journal is the decision that makes the byline public.

### Granting access

Also on the People screen. Use it sparingly.

Every change of access is permanently recorded in the activity log, including
who made it.

### Leadership for the year

Assign the Head of Research and Deputy Head of Research for an academic year.
This is separate from the permanent founding contributors, who are recorded once
and do not change from year to year.

## Reviewing research

*Leadership and school administration.* **Menu: Review research**

The screen lists submissions waiting for a decision. Open one to read the
abstract, download the document, and see any earlier versions and reviews.

### The two decisions

There are exactly two, and there is **no rejection**:

**Revision Required** — the work needs more before it can be approved.
**Feedback is required.** The system will not record this decision without it.

**Approved** — the work is ready to be considered for a journal.

Research is never rejected. Work that is not ready gets feedback and another
attempt.

### Writing feedback

The student reads your feedback on their own submission page. Write it for them:
say what needs to change and why. It is never written onto their document.

### What happens next

After **Revision Required**, the student uploads a new version. The old version
is kept — nothing is overwritten — and the new one comes back for review.

After **Approved**, the paper becomes available to be placed in a journal.

### Reviews are permanent

Once you record a decision, **it cannot be edited or deleted by anyone**. Not by
you, not by school administration, not by the student. The full history stays
attached to the work.

So read the submission before deciding, and re-read your feedback before
recording it.

## Building and publishing a journal

**Menu: Journals**

One journal per academic year. Leadership assembles it; **only school
administration can approve and publish it**.

### 1. Create it — *leadership*

Give it a title, a volume label and an academic year. It starts as a **draft**.

### 2. Assemble it — *leadership*

While it is a draft you can:

- **Add papers.** Only approved papers can be added.
- **Set the order** they appear in, with the up and down buttons.
- **Add credits** — editors, designers, teachers, anyone whose contribution
  should be recorded.
- **Upload a cover image and the journal PDF.**

Take your time here. Once it is submitted, this is all frozen.

### 3. Submit it for school approval — *leadership*

Click **Submit for school approval**. It moves to *awaiting school approval*, and
can no longer be edited.

You can **withdraw** it while it is waiting, which returns it to draft.

### 4. Approve and publish — *school administration*

School administration reviews it and publishes. **This is the moment it becomes
public**, and every paper in it becomes publicly readable with its authors'
names.

Check before publishing:

- every paper belongs in this journal
- the order is right
- the credits are complete and correctly spelled
- the cover and PDF are the final versions
- you are content for these students' names to be public

### Corrections after publication

A published journal is never edited. If something is wrong, record a
**correction**: it is added to the journal and displayed with it.

Both the original and the correction stay visible. That is how a journal of
record should behave — the correction is part of the history, not a way of
pretending the error never happened.

### Journal I

Journal I is already in the system as a historical record: its papers, its
authors, and everyone credited in it. Those students have **no accounts** — they
did not need one for their work to be recorded properly.

Do not delete it.

## Editing the site's wording

*School administration only.* **Menu: Site content**

The site's wording — tagline, mission statement, section introductions — is
edited here. Changes appear on the site **immediately**. Nobody technical needs
to be involved.

Some entries are marked **"Still a placeholder"**. These are pieces of wording
the school had not settled when the site was built.

**A placeholder is not shown to visitors.** The public page leaves it out
entirely rather than displaying `[PLACEHOLDER]` to the world. So an unfinished
tagline is invisible, not embarrassing — but it is also missing. This screen is
the list of what is still outstanding.

> The tagline in particular was left open on purpose. Whether the Research Cell's
> work is STEM-only had not been decided, and the site does not assert an answer
> until the school gives one.

## Announcements and resources

*School administration only.*

**Announcements** — short notices for the site. Set a start date and, optionally,
an end date. They appear and disappear on their own, so a notice about a deadline
can be written now and will stop showing after the deadline passes. Leave the end
date empty to show it indefinitely.

**Resources** — guidance, templates and referencing help. Each one stays a
**draft** until you tick *Publish this on the public Resources page*, so nothing
reaches the public before the school has agreed the wording.

## The activity log

*School administration only.* **Menu: Activity log**

A permanent record of administrative and editorial actions: accounts approved and
declined, access granted and revoked, statuses changed, profiles made public,
leadership assigned, research submitted and reviewed, journals created, approved,
published and corrected.

Each entry shows what happened, who did it, and when. Use the filters at the top
to narrow it down.

**Entries cannot be edited or removed by anyone — including school
administration.** They are written automatically as each action happens. This is
intentional: an audit log that its own administrators can edit is not an audit
log.

## At the start of a new academic year

A short checklist:

1. **Assign leadership for the year** — the new Head of Research and Deputy Head
   of Research (People screen).
2. **Mark leavers as alumni** rather than deleting them. Their research and their
   names stay on record.
3. **Approve the new members' accounts** as their registrations come in.
4. **Create the year's journal** when there is approved work to put in it.
5. **Review the site's wording** — check whether anything still marked as a
   placeholder can now be written.

## Common questions

**A student says they never got their invite link.**
Whoever approved the account has to send it; the system does not email it. If it
has been lost, ask a school administrator — the account already exists, and the
student can be sent a password reset from the sign-in page once email is set up,
or a new link can be generated.

**Can I undo a review decision?**
No. Reviews are permanent and cannot be edited or deleted by anyone. If you
recorded the wrong decision, the honest remedy is a further review on the next
version explaining the position.

**Can I delete a piece of research?**
A student can delete their own *draft*, before it is submitted. After that it
stays, because the review history is attached to it.

**A student left the school. Do I delete their account?**
Mark them **alumni**. Their name stays on their research, which is what you want.
Deleting the account would not remove their authorship — the two are stored
separately, on purpose — but alumni is the tidy way to do it.

**Can leadership publish a journal?**
No. Leadership assembles it and submits it. Only school administration approves
and publishes.

**Why can't I make my own suspended account active again?**
Because an account that can restore its own access is not really suspended.
Another school administrator has to do it.

**Something looks wrong and I do not want to make it worse.**
Nothing in the interface can destroy the archive: reviews, journals and the
activity log are all protected at a level the interface cannot reach. Stop, and
ask whoever maintains the site. If you have already published something you
should not have, record a **correction** rather than trying to undo it.
